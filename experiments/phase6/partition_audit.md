# Phase 6B: Pre-Training Data Partition Audit

Before designing the final training methodology, I audited the original dataset partitioning logic located in `src/split_data.py`. 

## 1. Origin of Partitions
The files `train_s1_ids.txt` and `val_s1_ids.txt` were generated by `src/split_data.py`. The script reads the original Amazon `train_source1.tsv` and `train_ground_truth.tsv` files, forming a strict 90/10 stratified split at the Source 1 entity level. The stratification ensures identical distributions of country, singleton status, and match-counts between training and validation.

## 2. Partition Row Counts
The original Amazon dataset `train_source1.tsv` contains exactly **2,206,821** labeled S1 entities.
The generated partitions contain:
* `experiments/train_s1_ids.txt`: **1,986,138** (90%)
* `experiments/val_s1_ids.txt`: **220,683** (10%)
* `experiments/dev_s1_ids.txt`: **1,500** 

## 3 & 4. Subsets and Disjoint Confirmations
* `train_s1_ids.txt` and `val_s1_ids.txt` are perfectly disjoint (intersection = 0). Together they precisely reconstruct the 2,206,821 original S1s.
* `dev_s1_ids.txt` is a strict, deterministic subset drawn from `val_s1_ids.txt`. (This explains why the earlier "train on dev, evaluate on val" produced a 0.6% data leakage into the 220,683 validation evaluation. Since it was only 1,500 out of 220,683 records, the metric skew was negligible, but it technically constitutes a methodological overlap).

## 5. Ground Truth Coverage
Yes, all 2,206,821 records possess verified labels in `train_ground_truth.tsv`.

## 6. Addressing the "542,909" Hallucination
In my previous plan, I incorrectly stated there were 542,909 training records. This was a complete hallucination—there are actually **1,986,138** fully valid training S1 records available. 
I proposed limiting the final training set to 50,000 because generating candidates and holding the feature matrix in memory for K=100 scales linearly. 
* 50,000 S1s × K=100 = 5,000,000 pairs ≈ 4.3 GB RAM.
* 1,986,138 S1s × K=100 = 198,613,800 pairs ≈ **165 GB RAM**.
Because the VM is hard-capped at 10GB of RAM, attempting to extract and load all training pairs simultaneously will instantly crash the machine.

## 7. Can we train on a larger deterministic sample?
Yes! Rather than discarding 95% of our 1.9M training records by subsampling to 50,000, we can utilize chunked incremental training.
XGBoost (`xgboost.train`) natively supports incremental learning (`xgb_model=previous_booster`). 
We can iterate through the 1.98M S1s in chunks (e.g., 20,000 S1s at a time):
1. Load 20,000 S1s.
2. Query blocker for candidates (2 million pairs).
3. Extract features in memory (~1.7 GB RAM).
4. Run XGBoost incremental update (`booster.update()`).
5. Free memory and proceed to the next chunk.

This allows us to train the final XGBoost matcher on the **entire 1,986,138-S1 training dataset** without ever exceeding ~2-3 GB of RAM, fully utilizing the 200 million candidate-pair distribution to map the absolute most robust non-linear boundaries before inference.
